# Editorial patterns

Use this reference for substantial user-facing prose. Treat the workspace `unslop/` package as upstream design input, not as packaged content for this skill.

## Prefer

- The result or current state in the first sentence.
- Concrete nouns, plain verbs, exact dates, identifiers, and measured effects already present in the evidence.
- One clear statement of uncertainty or limitation.
- Repeated terminology when changing the term could change or blur meaning.
- Uneven paragraph length when the content warrants it.
- A direct ending: the decision, blocker, approval needed, or next action.

## Remove or revise

- Ceremonial openings, conversational filler, hype, and claims of importance unsupported by evidence.
- Vague sources such as "experts" or "reports" when no source is named.
- Mechanical transitions, repeated summaries, and generic conclusion sections.
- Formulaic contrasts, forced sets of three, and slogans standing in for facts.
- Dense bolding, decorative headings, emoji, and tables that contain only one fact.
- Stock assurances such as "robust", "seamless", or "game-changing" when they add no testable meaning.

## Evidence discipline

Classify each substantive statement as one of:

- observed in a named source or live system;
- derived from an explicit assumption or calculation;
- professional judgment with its basis;
- an unresolved evidence gap.

Do not upgrade one class into another during editing. Preserve qualifiers that affect correctness, but avoid repeating the same qualifier sentence by sentence.

## Final pass

Confirm that:

- every concrete detail is traceable;
- no technical token or required field changed;
- status language matches the evidence;
- the response does not repeat its opening at the end;
- tables and lists improve scanning instead of fragmenting connected prose;
- the shortest version that preserves the necessary facts, risks, and actions was used.
